CLIENT STATEMENT
Matter: Hartley v Pemberton – boundary fence (MAT-2026-014)
Statement of: Mr Robert Hartley, 14 Orchard Lane, Fenbridge
Taken by: Priya Shah, Wakefield & Croft LLP
Date: 26 August 2026

1. My wife Susan and I bought 14 Orchard Lane in March 2009. At that time the boundary with number 12 was marked along most of its length by a low beech hedge. At the rear of the gardens there was no hedge, only a line of old fence posts with no panels between them.

2. In spring 2011 our then neighbour, Mrs Dorothy Ellis, put up a wooden panel fence at the rear of the gardens. I remember this because it was the same month our daughter was born. We did not object at the time because Mrs Ellis told us she was only replacing the old posts and keeping her dog in.

3. In 2018 I noticed that the 2011 fence did not follow the line of the old posts. At the rear it seemed to bend into our garden. I measured it and thought it was about 30 centimetres onto our side at the very back.

4. On 3 May 2018 I wrote to Mrs Ellis about this. I said that I did not accept that the fence was on the boundary and that I was objecting to her use of the strip behind it. I kept a copy of the letter, which I have given to Priya Shah. Mrs Ellis replied by card saying she was sorry and that she would "sort it out with the next fence". Nothing was done before she sold the house.

5. Mr Pemberton bought number 12 in 2019. In June 2026 he took down the 2011 fence and put up a new, taller close-boarded fence. The new fence is further into our garden again than the 2011 fence. At the rear corner I measured the new fence at about 40 centimetres from the line of the old posts, some of which are still visible in the ground on our side.

6. I have photographs of the old posts taken in 2009 and 2018, and photographs of the 2011 fence taken in 2018, before it was removed.

7. We would like the fence moved back to the line of the old posts. We are willing to try mediation but we do not want to give up the strip.

I believe the facts stated in this statement are true.

Robert Hartley
